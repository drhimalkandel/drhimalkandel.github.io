Kornhauser Research Associate
Save Sight Institute · Sydney Medical School · Faculty of Medicine and Health · The University of Sydney

I am a vision and outcomes researcher with a particular interest in patient-reported outcomes, quality of life, corneal disease, keratoconus, refractive error and real-world clinical registry research.

My research aims to understand not only clinical outcomes, but also how eye diseases and their treatments affect patients' daily lives, well-being and experiences of care.

About

I am based at the Save Sight Institute, The University of Sydney, where my work spans clinical outcomes research, patient-reported outcomes, corneal disease and real-world evidence.

My research uses clinical registry data, statistical modelling, psychometric methods and qualitative and mixed-methods approaches to evaluate outcomes that matter to both patients and clinicians.

I collaborate with clinicians and researchers in Australia and internationally, and contribute to research supervision, mentoring, scientific communication and community engagement.

Research Focus

Outcomes Research

Evaluation of clinical and patient-reported outcomes using longitudinal data, statistical modelling and real-world evidence.

Quality of Life

Development and application of patient-reported outcome measures, including modern psychometric methods such as Rasch analysis and item-response theory.

Cornea & Keratoconus

Research into keratoconus, corneal cross-linking, disease progression, treatment outcomes and the patient impact of corneal disease.

Clinical Registries

Use of real-world clinical registry data to study effectiveness, safety, treatment variation and patient outcomes in routine clinical practice.

Research Recognition

Award-Winning Research on Dry Eye Disease

My research examining the impact of dry eye disease on patient-reported quality of life received the University of Sydney Faculty of Medicine and Health EMCR Outstanding Publication Award in the Mid-Career category.

The research has also attracted professional media coverage and was featured in the Faculty's Research Bytes podcast.
