Research Supervision

I have supervised and mentored research students across higher degree, postgraduate and undergraduate programs in ophthalmology, optometry, vision science and related health research.

My supervision focuses on developing independent researchers, strengthening research methodology and supporting students through the full research process, from project development to publication and dissemination.

Supervision Experience

My research supervision spans a range of academic levels and project types, including higher degree research, postgraduate coursework research, clinical research projects and undergraduate research.

Higher Degree Research

Supervision and mentoring of research candidates undertaking advanced projects in vision science, ophthalmology and clinical research.

Postgraduate Research

Supervision of postgraduate research projects involving clinical outcomes, evidence synthesis and patient-centred research.

Clinical Research Projects

Guidance on projects using clinical data, registries and real-world evidence to investigate patient outcomes and treatment effectiveness.

Undergraduate Research

Mentoring students in research design, data interpretation, scientific writing and presentation of research findings.

Areas of Supervision

Cornea & Keratoconus

Research involving keratoconus, corneal cross-linking, disease progression, treatment outcomes and corneal disease.

Clinical Registries

Registry-based research, real-world clinical data, outcome evaluation and translation of evidence into practice.

Patient-Reported Outcomes

Development, validation and application of patient-reported outcome measures and patient-centred research methods.

Quality of Life

Research examining how eye diseases and treatments affect daily functioning, well-being and quality of life.

Dry Eye & Ocular Surface

Clinical and patient-centred research related to dry eye disease and ocular surface conditions.

Evidence Synthesis

Systematic reviews, meta-analyses and evidence-based research addressing clinically relevant questions in eye care.

Mentoring Approach

My approach to supervision is collaborative, structured and focused on developing research independence.

I support students in refining research questions, selecting appropriate methodologies, analysing and interpreting data, preparing manuscripts and presenting findings to academic and clinical audiences.

I also encourage students to develop broader research skills, including critical thinking, scientific communication, project management and academic confidence.

Research Training

Research training is an important part of my academic work. I contribute to the development of emerging researchers through supervision, mentoring, research discussions, manuscript development and support with conference presentations and publication.

My aim is to help students develop both the technical skills required for high-quality research and the broader capabilities needed to become independent and collaborative researchers.

Related Research

The research areas represented in my supervision align closely with my broader research program in outcomes research, corneal disease, clinical registries, quality of life and patient-reported outcomes.
