Research

My research focuses on patient-centred outcomes in eye care, with particular interests in patient-reported outcomes, quality of life, corneal disease, keratoconus, refractive error and real-world clinical registry research.

I use quantitative, qualitative and mixed-methods approaches to understand how eye conditions and their treatments affect patients, and to generate evidence that can support better clinical decision-making and patient-centred care.

Research Areas

1 Outcomes Research

A major focus of my work is the evaluation of both clinical outcomes and patient-reported outcomes. My research includes longitudinal data analysis, statistical modelling and analysis of real-world clinical data to better understand treatment effectiveness and patient outcomes.

2 Patient-Reported Outcomes & Quality of Life

I have a longstanding research interest in measuring how eye diseases and treatments affect people's daily lives and well-being. My work includes development, validation and application of patient-reported outcome measures using modern psychometric approaches.

This research incorporates qualitative, quantitative and mixed-methods designs, including Rasch analysis and item-response theory.

3 Cornea & Keratoconus

My corneal research has a particular focus on keratoconus and real-world outcomes following corneal cross-linking. This includes research into disease progression, treatment effectiveness, clinical predictors, quality of life and patient-reported outcomes.

I work with the Save Sight Keratoconus Registry, which provides real-world clinical data to evaluate outcomes of keratoconus management across clinical practice.

4 Refractive Error

My research in refractive error focuses on understanding the impact of refractive error and its correction on quality of life.

My PhD research developed item banks designed to measure refractive error-specific quality-of-life outcomes and the benefits of interventions such as spectacles, contact lenses and refractive surgery.

5 Clinical Registries & Real-World Evidence

Clinical registries provide an opportunity to study treatment effectiveness and patient outcomes in routine clinical practice. My work includes the analysis and implementation of registry-based research, particularly through the Save Sight Registries.

I am interested in using real-world evidence to complement traditional clinical research and improve the translation of research findings into practice.

Research Methods

My research uses a range of methodological approaches depending on the research question and the type of outcome being evaluated.

Clinical Data Analysis

Longitudinal data analysis, statistical modelling and analysis of real-world clinical registry data.

Psychometrics

Rasch analysis, item-response theory and development and validation of patient-reported outcome measures.

Mixed Methods

Qualitative, quantitative and mixed-methods approaches for investigating patient experiences and outcomes.

Save Sight Registries

A substantial component of my current research is based on real-world clinical data from the Save Sight Registries.

My work with the Save Sight Keratoconus Registry includes registry implementation, research publications, analysis of clinical and patient-reported outcome data, and investigation of treatment outcomes in keratoconus.

Registry research allows outcomes to be evaluated across routine clinical practice and provides evidence about effectiveness, safety and variation in patient outcomes.

International & Collaborative Research

My research includes collaborations with researchers and clinical groups in Australia and internationally.

International Myopia Institute

Collaborative research examining the global impact of myopia, including its individual, societal and quality-of-life burden.

BCLA CLEAR

Contributions to international evidence-based reports in contact lenses and presbyopia through the British Contact Lens Association CLEAR initiative.

Global Burden of Disease

Collaboration on large international studies investigating disease burden, vision impairment and broader global health outcomes.

Corneal Research

Collaborative research with clinicians and researchers investigating keratoconus, corneal cross-linking, dry eye disease and other corneal conditions.

Research Translation & Engagement

An important part of my research is translating evidence beyond academic publications. My activities include engagement with patients, clinicians, researchers and professional organisations.

This has included educational and community initiatives focused on keratoconus, clinical registries and patient-reported outcomes.
